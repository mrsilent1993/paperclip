# YouTube Discovery & SEO Playbook (2025–2026) for a New Faceless "What If" History Channel

Research date: 2026-11-14. Labels used: **[OFFICIAL]** = YouTube/Google statement or Help Center; **[DATA]** = third-party dataset (usually vendor research, correlational); **[LORE]** = creator/blog advice without published data. Most quantitative studies come from tool vendors (vidIQ, Metricool, AIR, Spotter, 1of10) that sell to creators. Treat them as directional.

## 1. How the recommendation system works (official statements) and how new channels get first impressions

### Takeaway
YouTube's own statements say recommendations follow audience response, measured as satisfaction rather than raw clicks or minutes. Suggested (watch page) is the largest surface even for subscribers, and the system can match a new channel's videos to viewers by topic before the channel has any history. It does not publish traffic-source shares for new channels, and it gives no fixed "test window".

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] The "How YouTube Works" recommendations page names viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback "including from satisfaction surveys" as inputs. Watch Next/Up Next "suggests additional content based on what you're currently watching". The homepage blends personalised recommendations, subscriptions and news. The page also says the system weighs "the reputation and the quality of a channel" — [YouTube, How YouTube Works: Recommendations](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)
- [OFFICIAL] Todd Beaupré (Senior Director, Growth & Discovery) on Creator Insider, published 1 Sep 2026: "No metric on its own is a good indicator of value". "We don't want to just assume that all time is valuable". Surveys separate time spent from satisfaction — [PPC Land summary of Creator Insider interview](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [OFFICIAL] Same interview: the subscriptions traffic source typically has "about maybe a 10% or less click-through rate", so "even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch". "The number one place that people are watching videos from channels they're subscribed to" is the suggested list on the watch page. The subscriptions feed is "quite small by comparison" — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [OFFICIAL] Same interview: for new channels, parts of the system work without viewer history and match topics against what a video is about. Creators should "align channels with audiences" and use separate channels only when audience expectations differ. PPC Land notes that none of the claims came with published data — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [OFFICIAL] Same interview: Beaupré described a test that capped recommending a given video at eight times — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Rene Ritchie (Creator Liaison): "The algorithm follows the audience, so please the audience". Check the Audience tab to see which videos grow the audience. Putting a video in front of the "right fit viewer" is "a faster, better way to get more impressions and data on that channel" — [Buffer guide quoting Ritchie](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-algorithm/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/401206/youtube-liaison-opens-up-about-the-algorithm.html)
- [OFFICIAL, unverified secondhand] Ritchie reportedly said on Threads that YouTube reserves a slot or two at the top of Home for videos from new channels. The original post was not verified — [KDCC blog](https://blog.kdcc.social/youtube-home-page-recommendations-new-channels/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2023] Ritchie: "The thumbnail makes a promise, and the video has to deliver on it". "The first act has to be a reward". "you can't do the slow burn". He recommended end-of-video cliffhangers that make viewers want the next video — [Search Engine Journal, Aug 2023](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)
- [LORE] Blogs claim a new-channel "test period" of 10–15 videos before Suggested traffic appears, or extended evaluation up to 30–50 videos. These figures conflict, and YouTube publishes no such window — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-algorithm-new-channels); [Voclify](https://voclify.io/blog/youtube-recommendation-algorithm-new-channels-2026); [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/new-creators-youtube/)

### Inferences
- A faceless "What if" channel should be built for the Suggested surface: position videos next to established history/alt-history videos the target viewer already watches, using recognizable topics such as "What if Rome never fell". Search is a secondary entry point, and Subscriptions is minor.
- Satisfaction (surveys, likes, return visits) and keeping the promise of the packaging matter more than clickbait CTR. Click-bait-then-disappoint likely suppresses distribution.
- A pivot is costly ("align channels with audiences"). Pick the niche ("counterfactual history") and stay in it from video one.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 breakdown of Browse vs Suggested vs Search share for new channels was found. Typical shares have to come from the channel's own Analytics.
- The new-channel Home slot claim has only a secondhand source.

## 2. Titles & thumbnails: CTR benchmarks, curiosity vs clarity, faces, Test & Compare

### Takeaway
The only official benchmark is that half of channels/videos have an impressions CTR of 2–10%. CTR naturally drops as reach widens. YouTube's native Test & Compare now covers titles and thumbnails (up to 3 variants) and picks winners by watch time share, not CTR. Data suggests faces are not necessary, which suits a faceless channel.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary] Half of all channels/videos have impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. CTR varies by content, audience and surface. Small fluctuations are normal, and you should wait for enough impressions before judging CTR — [YouTube Help: Impressions & CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154); [YouTube Help: Decoding CTR & impressions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369?hl=en); summarized in [Focus Digital 2026](https://focus-digital.co/average-youtube-ctr-organic-paid-benchmarks-2026/)
- [OFFICIAL] Test & Compare: test "Title only", "Thumbnail only" or "Title and thumbnail", up to 3 variants. The winner is chosen by highest watch time share ("optimizes for watch time rather than click-through rate"). Results are Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive, and an inconclusive test keeps the first-uploaded option. Tests should complete within about 2 weeks. The feature is desktop Studio only, needs advanced features, and excludes Shorts, scheduled lives, Premieres (until they end), made-for-kids and private videos. Use visibly different variants, upload at ≥1280×720, and note that editing the title or thumbnail mid-test stops it. YouTube suggests testing older videos first — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)
- [DATA/news] Title testing rolled out globally in 2025. Reports differ on exact timing (mid-2025 vs December 2025) — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-expands-a-b-testing-to-include-titles-alongside-thumbnails/)
- [DATA] A 1of10 dataset of about 300K viral videos found that thumbnails with faces perform about the same as those without on average. Differences are larger by niche, and faces help bigger channels more than small ones. 1of10 says education/tech lean neutral, where "packaging and text drive the click, not the face" — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/); [1of10 blog](https://1of10.com/blog/should-you-use-your-face-in-your-youtube-thumbnails)
- [DATA, conflicting] A "Richard the YouTube Strategist" 2025 study is cited as finding faces 36% better. This conflicts with 1of10 and was not verified at source — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2023] Ritchie retargeted a video to a broader audience by changing its title and thumbnail. Packaging defines which audience the system tests — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)

### Inferences
- Run Test & Compare on every long-form upload with 3 visibly different concepts, for example a map plus an alternate flag, a dramatic scene, or a "then vs now" split. Because the winner is decided by watch time, curiosity titles that the video doesn't pay off will lose. Aim for "curiosity anchored in a clear, known topic", e.g. "What If the Mongols Conquered Europe?"
- For faceless history, a strong single focal image (a historical figure's painted face counts as a face), high contrast, and 0–4 words of text that complement rather than repeat the title. This is lore-level design advice; the face evidence is neutral.
- Judge CTR against your own channel and per surface. A falling CTR during a breakout is expected.

### Gaps
- No credible 2025–2026 study on optimal title length or text-on-thumbnail for education/history specifically was found. Paddy Galloway's published research was not located in this pass.

## 3. Metadata: description, tags, chapters, captions, hashtags, keyword research, Google "key moments"

### Takeaway
Officially, titles, thumbnails and descriptions are the metadata that matter. Tags "play a minimal role" except for common misspellings, so older "tag stuffing" advice is outdated and can breach spam policy. Chapters can surface as Google Key Moments, but only on videos that already rank.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Tags "play a minimal role in your video's discovery" except for commonly misspelled topics. Titles, thumbnails and descriptions are "more important pieces of metadata". Excessive tags in descriptions violate spam/deceptive practices policies — [YouTube Help 146402](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402?hl=en)
- [DATA/blog] YouTube chapters (first at 0:00, ≥3 timestamps) can appear as Google "Key Moments". Google favours creator-set chapters and uses chapter titles as labels. Studio has "Allow automatic chapters and key moments", on by default. Key Moments do not raise ranking and appear only on videos that already rank — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-chapters-key-moments/); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-chapters-as-an-seo-tool-when-they-help-when-they-hurt)
- [DATA/blog] Trade-off: chapters let viewers skip the setup, which may hurt retention on narrative videos — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-chapters-as-an-seo-tool-when-they-help-when-they-hurt)
- [OFFICIAL] Beaupré says the system matches topics to what a video is about for new channels. Clear, topic-explicit metadata (title, description, spoken script, which feeds captions) therefore helps the cold-start match — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

### Inferences
- Description: 1–2 natural sentences up front restating the topic with its key entities (e.g. "What if the Byzantine Empire survived 1453? An alternate history of Constantinople…"), then chapters and sources. Tags: 3–5 including misspellings (e.g. "Byzantine/Bysantine"). Hashtags: up to 3 (e.g. #history #alternatehistory). Upload accurate captions (script-based) because a faceless narration is fully transcribable.
- Keyword research: use YouTube search autocomplete ("what if the…", "what would happen if…") and vidIQ/TubeBuddy volume/competition scores as directional tools. Search is a steadier evergreen source for "what if" queries than Browse.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 primary study quantifying the ranking effect of captions, hashtags or description length was found. The official Help page does not explain hashtag discovery weight.
- vidIQ/TubeBuddy keyword-score accuracy has no independent validation.

## 4. Upload cadence, consistency, and video length

### Takeaway
Large vendor datasets associate 4–7 long-form uploads per month and a predictable schedule with faster growth, but all of it is correlational. For new channels, each additional upload is mainly another lottery ticket for a breakout. Long-form views per view session are shrinking, while long videos (30+ min) dominate TV/US viewing time.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] vidIQ (10.2M channels ≥1K subs, Apr 2025–Mar 2026): channels with 4–7 uploads/month had 1.32% median monthly view growth, about 2.5× the 0.53% of channels with <1 upload/month. Correlational; bigger channels can afford more uploads — [vidIQ Upload Frequency Study, July 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/)
- [DATA] AIR (18,000 channels): predictable schedules (low variance in gaps) got more views per subscriber than the same volume posted irregularly. Going dark for 14+ days three or more times a year correlated with fewer views per video, more so for small channels. Correlational — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/does-posting-more-on-youtube-help-we-studied-18000-channels-to-find-out)
- [DATA] OverseerOS (2,543 gaps, 53 channels): shorter gaps did not increase per-video breakout probability. No universal number — [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-upload-frequency-study)
- [DATA] vidIQ first-10-videos study (channels created Jan–Jun 2025 with ≥10 same-format uploads in 90 days; 137,355 long-form and 367,243 Shorts channels): median 30-day views of long-form uploads rose from about 180 (1st) to about 211 (5th) to about 272 (10th), +51%. The best of the first 10 long-form videos had a median of about 1,948 views (about 10.8× the first). 57.8% of long-form channels had at least one of their first 10 reach 1K views, and 30.8% reached 10K. Per video, 30.2% of long-form uploads reached 1K. Observational, with survivorship bias (only channels that reached 10 uploads) — [vidIQ Research, July 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- [DATA] Metricool (799,718 videos, Feb 2025 vs Feb 2026): long-form average view duration fell 37% (3.98 → 2.51 min), while total long-form watch time rose 11% and long-form views rose 76% — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-views-are-rising-but-watch-time-and-ad-revenue-are-falling-study-finds/); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-has-more-views-but-viewers-are-watching-less/)
- [DATA] Digital i: share of US YouTube viewing time on videos of 30+ minutes rose from 65% (Oct 2023) to 73% (Oct 2024) — [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/02/06/data-long-form-content-takes-73-of-youtube-us-viewing-time)
- [DATA] Little Dot whitepaper (Feb 2026) reports the average length of suggested/recommended videos shifted from 80 to 40 minutes. Baseline and methodology unverified — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026)
- [OFFICIAL] Beaupré said the desktop homepage redesign increased long-form attention and engagement ("Often less is more") — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

### Inferences
- A realistic target for a new faceless history channel is 1–2 long-form videos per week (4–8/month), held steady. Reach 10 long-form uploads within about 60–90 days, the threshold the vidIQ cohort used. Expect early medians in the hundreds of views; the breakout distribution is what matters.
- Length: let the story determine it. 10–20 minutes is a common practical band for "What if" scripts, and longer pieces (25–40+ min) suit TV viewing. Optimize retention, not length. (The band is inference, not sourced.)

### Gaps
- No credible study on optimal length specifically for history/documentary niches was found.

## 5. Shorts: help or hurt long-form? Linking Shorts to long videos

### Takeaway
Officially, YouTube separates Shorts and long-form audiences and says mixing formats doesn't harm a channel. Data shows Shorts get early views more easily for new channels, but the Shorts "related video" link converts poorly (often <1%). Modest Shorts volume (about 5/month) correlates with better long-form views, and posting Shorts within 3 days of a long-form upload helps most.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Creator Insider: Shorts and long-form viewers aren't always the same people, so YouTube separates Shorts and long-form watch history for recommendations. YouTube has said posting both formats on one channel doesn't harm performance — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm/543939/); [Pandavideo](https://www.pandavideo.com/blog/shorts-and-long-form-videos-same-channel)
- [DATA] vidIQ first-10 study: the median first Short got about 1,362 30-day views vs about 180 for a first long-form video. 86.5% of Shorts channels had a Short reach 1K among their first 10 (vs 57.8% long-form). Shorts views did not grow from upload 1 to 10, while long-form views grew 51% — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- [DATA] Spotter (5,000+ channels): medium and large channels with notable Related-Shorts traffic saw about +10% first-30-day views on new long-form uploads vs channel average. Shorts posted within 3 days of the long-form upload drove more long-form views — [Spotter "The Spot"](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/related-shorts)
- [DATA] Metricool: channels posting about 5 Shorts/month averaged 8,789 views per long-form video (46% above average). The lift disappears beyond about 35 Shorts/month. Correlational — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/how-to-grow-on-youtube/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-views-are-rising-but-watch-time-and-ad-revenue-are-falling-study-finds/)
- [LORE] The related-video link converts "a few hundred viewers per 100,000 Short views" (Outlier Kit) or "sometimes under 1%" (Subscribr). Pinned comments and playlists are claimed to do better — [Outlier Kit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-to-long-form/); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)
- [DATA, 2023] An FT-cited internal YouTube concern: creators were uploading fewer long-form videos as Shorts grew — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/09/05/youtube-shorts-cannibalizing-long-form-views/)
- [DATA] Net Influencer reports some creators' long-form averages fell (e.g. ~150K → ~60K per upload) as distribution tilted toward Shorts. Anecdotal, not platform-wide — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-distribution-is-tilting-toward-shorts-pressuring-long-form-reach-data-suggests/)

### Inferences
- Use Shorts as trailers and cliffhangers cut from each long-form video ("What if Napoleon won Waterloo? Part 1 → full video linked"): 2–3 per long-form video, posted within 72 hours, each with the Related Video link set. Don't let Shorts volume crowd out long-form production; Shorts subscribers often won't watch long-form.

### Gaps
- No independent or official conversion-rate figure for the Related Video link was found.

## 6. Playlists/series, end screens, pinned comments, Posts, Premieres, Hype

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin for most of these. The notable 2025 addition is "Hype": a viewer-powered boost for channels under 500K subscribers that can push a video onto an Explore leaderboard, weighted toward smaller channels.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL/news] Hype launched globally in Aug 2025 for channels under 500K subscribers. Each viewer can hype up to 3 videos per week, points push videos up an Explore leaderboard, and points are weighted to favour smaller channels (exact multipliers undisclosed). The beta saw 5M+ hypes across 50K+ channels — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/26/youtubes-hype-feature-that-boosts-smaller-creators-launches-globally); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/26/youtube-hype-feature-launches-united-states-global/); [Outlier Kit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hype-feature-small-creators-growth-2026/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2023] Ritchie recommends cliffhangers at the end of videos to pull viewers into the next video, which supports series and end-screen design — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)
- [OFFICIAL] Test & Compare does not work on Premieres until they end — [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] Because Suggested is the top surface, being the natural "next video" matters. This argues for series and playlists (e.g. "What If: Ancient Empires") — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

### Inferences
- Build themed series playlists. End each video with a cliffhanger that teases a specific next "What if" and points the end screen at it. Pin a comment that poses a debate question, which drives comments and satisfaction. Ask viewers to Hype once a small core audience exists. Premieres can wait until a core audience exists, since they block testing during the premiere.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 quantitative data was found on end-screen CTR, pinned comments, Posts (community) or Premieres. These were not covered by the searches run.

## 7. Multi-language audio / auto-dubbing as a growth lever

### Takeaway
YouTube expanded multi-language audio to millions of creators in Sept 2025 and auto-dubbing (AI) to broad availability. YouTube-reported pilot data shows more than 25% of watch time coming from non-primary-language views. This is a cheap, potentially large lever for narration-only faceless channels.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL via news] Multi-language audio opened to millions of creators in Sept 2025. Creators using it saw over 25% of watch time from non-primary-language views. Jamie Oliver's channel saw 3× views vs English-only (single case) — [Business Today, Sept 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/youtube-launches-multi-language-audio-for-millions-of-creators-extending-global-reach-with-auto-dubbing-493458-2025-09-11); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-multi-language-audio-available-to-millions-of-creators/)
- [OFFICIAL, 2023] Early multi-language audio pilot: about 15% of watch time came from non-primary-language views — [9to5Google, Feb 2023](https://9to5google.com/2023/02/23/youtube-multi-language-audio/)
- [DATA/blog] Auto-dubbing (AI, inside YouTube) is distinct from creator-uploaded audio tracks. One 2026 explainer says it covers 27 languages, 8 with "Expressive Speech". Not verified with Google — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-multi-language-audio/); [Outlier Kit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-auto-dubbing-all-creators-global-growth-2026/)

### Inferences
- Faceless narration has no lip-sync problem, so dubbing fits the format well. Start with high-volume languages (Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, etc.), review auto-dub quality on the first videos, and translate titles and descriptions too. Measure per-language results in Studio.

### Gaps
- Claims of "20–35% watch-time lift within 90 days" come from vendor blogs and were not verified. No data was found for small or new channels specifically.

## 8. A benchmarked 90-day plan for new channels

### Takeaway
No credible source publishes an official 90-day plan. The best benchmark is vidIQ's 2025 cohort study, which gives realistic first-90-day medians for channels that publish ≥10 videos. Any plan should be built from those numbers.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] Benchmarks for long-form channels with ≥10 uploads in their first 90 days: median 30-day views of about 180 (1st video), about 272 (10th), about 1,948 (best of first 10). 57.8% get a 1K-view video, and 30.8% get a 10K-view video — [vidIQ Research, July 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- [DATA] 4–7 uploads/month correlates with about 2.5× monthly view growth vs <1/month, and consistent scheduling correlates with more views per subscriber — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/); [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/does-posting-more-on-youtube-help-we-studied-18000-channels-to-find-out)
- [LORE] Blog-level claims that Suggested traffic appears after 10–15 videos — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-algorithm-new-channels)

### Inferences (draft plan, not a sourced benchmark)
- Days 1–30: publish 4 long-form videos (weekly, same day) on the most-searched "What if" topics, plus 2–3 Shorts per video within 72 hours, linked. Run Test & Compare on each upload, add chapters and captions, and keep 2–3 series playlists.
- Days 31–60: 4–6 more long-form videos. Double down on the topic and packaging style of the best performer (check the Audience tab and Suggested sources). Enable auto-dubbing on the best videos.
- Days 61–90: reach 12+ long-form videos. Make a sequel or follow-up to any breakout, and start asking for Hype. Success = at least one video at ≥1K views (beats the median channel) and ideally one at ≥10K (top about 30%).

### Gaps
- No independent, non-vendor 90-day benchmark exists. Subscriber benchmarks at day 90 were not found.
