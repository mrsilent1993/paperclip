# Viewing Habits of English-Speaking Audiences (US/UK/CA/AU) for History, "What If", Explainer and Educational YouTube Content (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Notes on source quality are inline. "Hard data" = official platform statements, measurement bodies (Nielsen, Ofcom, OzTAM, Numeris), or Pew. "Vendor/creator data" = tool vendors (vidIQ, OutlierKit, Retention Rabbit), MCNs (Little Dot), or blogs. I found no source that publishes an audience profile *specifically* for history/"what if" YouTube viewers. Most demographic data below is platform-wide and serves only as a proxy.

## 1. Demographics (age, gender, countries, income/education)

### Takeaway
YouTube reaches about 85% of US adults, with more than 90% of under-50s using it and 86% of men versus 83% of women. No published dataset breaks out history or educational viewers. The best proxies point to a male skew and a 25–44 core: men make up 57% of YouTube news consumers, and 25–44 men are about 22% of YouTube's global ad audience. Income and education splits for this niche are not published.

### Cited Findings
- **Hard data (US, Pew 2024 survey, published Feb 2025):** 85% of US adults use YouTube. Use is above 90% for under-50s, 86% for ages 50–64 and 65% for 65+. — [Pew Research, 5 facts about Americans and YouTube (Feb 2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/02/28/5-facts-about-americans-and-youtube/)
- **Hard data (US, Pew 2025 survey, n=5,022, Feb–Jun 2025):** 86% of men and 83% of women use YouTube. A majority of every age group uses YouTube, and the youngest adults are the most likely to. 33% of adults visit YouTube several times a day. — [Pew, Americans' Social Media Use 2025 (Nov 2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/)
- **Hard data (US, Pew):** Among people who get news on YouTube, 57% are men and 42% are women. 32% of US adults regularly got news on YouTube in 2024, up from 23% in 2020. Of the "news influencers" on YouTube, 68% are men. — [Pew, Feb 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/02/28/5-facts-about-americans-and-youtube/)
- **Platform-wide, global (Statista, as of April 2024):** Men aged 25–34 are 12.1% of YouTube's global audience and men aged 35–44 are 9.8%, about 22% combined. Statista notes these are ad-tool figures that may not represent unique individuals. — [Statista via search summary](https://statista.com/statistics/1287137/youtube-global-users-age-gender-distribution)
- **Platform-wide gender (2024):** About 54% of the global audience is male. — [Oberlo](https://oberlo.com/statistics/youtube-gender-demographics); [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/youtube-users)
- **Older category data (OpenSlate, via Digiday, about 2017–2018):** Men were the majority of viewers in 90% of the 51 YouTube content categories measured. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/platforms/demographics-youtube-5-charts/)
- **Learning motive (older, 2017):** 86% of US viewers said they often use YouTube to learn new things (2and2/Google "Values of YouTube" study, n=1,006, ages 18–54, Oct 2017). — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/data/youtube-learning-statistics)
- **Learning motive (secondhand, 2025):** A Kantar survey reportedly found 93% of US 18–27-year-olds say YouTube helps them learn new skills. I could not verify this against the primary Kantar source. — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtubes-5-5-billion-views-how-the-platform-is-reshaping-classroom-learning/)
- **Canada (trade press on Numeris, Sep–Nov 2025):** YouTube was Canada's most-watched streaming platform and led among adults 18–44, ahead of Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ and Crave on total reach. — [Marketing News Canada](https://marketingnewscanada.com/news/youtube-is-canadas-1-streaming-platform-heres-what-that-means-for-your-media-plan); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/television/news/youtube-tops-canadian-streaming-tv-viewership-in-fall-2025-beats-netflix-and-disney-eee49db5-ae0d-4674-b198-5f3877f6d560/)
- **Australia (OzTAM Streamscape Q3 2025):** YouTube took 20.5% of streaming minutes among people aged 25–54, just behind Netflix at 21.3%. — [OzTAM Streamscape Q3 2025](https://oztam.com.au/news-update/demographic-insights-expand-streamscape-q3-2025/)
- **UK (Ofcom Media Nations 2025, 2024 data):** YouTube is the UK's second most-watched service after the BBC, ahead of ITV. Over-55s nearly doubled their YouTube viewing on TV sets year on year. — [Ofcom Media Nations 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/media-nations-2025); [ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/07/ofcom-youtube-and-streaming-is-slowly-taking-over-uk-tv-sets.html)

### Inferences
- The likely core audience for history and "what if" content is men aged 25–54, with a meaningful 55+ tail that is growing on TV sets (Ofcom). This rests on proxies: male skew among YouTube news consumers, men's majority across most categories, and history's overlap with documentary and news-adjacent interest. A new channel should check its own YouTube Studio data after its first 10–20 uploads.
- Because older viewers are adopting YouTube fastest on the living-room TV, deliberately paced 8–15 minute narrated videos may suit them better than fast-cut formats.
- Among English-speaking markets, the US will dominate views by sheer size. The UK, Canada and Australia are each smaller but are high-ad-rate (CPM) secondary markets.

### Gaps
- I found no published age, gender, income or education breakdown specific to history or educational YouTube viewers in any of the four countries. Pew does not break YouTube use out by genre beyond news.
- I found no 2024–2026 income or education data for YouTube users. Pew's fact sheet includes it, but I did not retrieve those rows.
- I found no direct country-share data showing what percentage of an English history channel's views come from the US, UK, Canada and Australia.

## 2. Devices: TV/CTV vs mobile vs desktop

### Takeaway
The TV is now YouTube's primary screen in the US and a fast-growing one in the UK, Australia and Canada. YouTube says more than half of US watch time is on TV, and Nielsen ranks YouTube the #1 distributor on US TVs at about 12–13% of all TV time. Thumbnails, on-screen text and pacing should be designed for a 10-foot living-room view as well as for phones.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube official (Feb 2025 CEO letter):** In the US, TV overtook mobile as the #1 device for YouTube watch time. Viewers watch more than 1 billion hours of YouTube a day on living-room TVs worldwide. — [Tubefilter on Neal Mohan's 2025 letter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/11/neal-mohan-youtube-letter-2025-youtube-tv-watch-time-creator-studio-businesses/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/youtube-more-people-watch-tv-vs-mobile)
- **YouTube official, via trade press (May 2026):** More than 50% of US YouTube watch time happens on TV screens, about 200 million hours a day in US living rooms. — [MarketBeat on Mohan remarks, May 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/alphabets-youtube-ceo-touts-ai-creators-and-tv-as-growth-flywheel-2026-05-17/)
- **Nielsen (US) hard data:** YouTube reached 12.4% of all US TV viewing in April 2025, a record, up from 11.6% in February. It led all media distributors through 2025, averaging about 11.8% from January to August. — [Nielsen, Feb 2025 Media Distributor Gauge](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/youtube-achieves-best-monthly-performance-to-date-and-pulls-ahead-in-nielsens-february-media-distributor-gauge/); [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2025/06/25/youtube-april-2025-distributor-gauge/)
- **Ofcom (UK, 2024 data, published July 2025):** People spent 39 minutes a day on YouTube at home, 16 of them on the TV set. The TV share of in-home YouTube viewing rose from 34% in 2023 to 41% in 2024. — [Ofcom Media Nations 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/media-nations-2025)
- **Ofcom (UK, 2025 data, Media Nations 2026):** Average daily YouTube viewing on TV sets grew from 9 minutes per person in 2022 to 19 minutes in 2025. — [Ofcom Media Nations 2026 report (PDF)](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/multi-sector/media-nations/2026/media-nations-2026-uk-report.pdf)
- **OzTAM (Australia, Q2 2026):** YouTube held 11.3% of all video viewing on TV sets, up 0.9 points on the previous quarter, with growth in every major demographic. — [OzTAM Streamscape Q2 2026 (PDF)](https://oztam.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Streamscape-Q2-2026_Total-TV-retains-majority-share-as-viewing-continues-to-evolve.pdf)
- **Little Dot Studios whitepaper (MCN, 800+ channels), Feb 2026:** Long-form channels earn a higher share of watch time and revenue from YouTube Premium subscribers. One reason given is that ad breaks on connected TVs are long and often unskippable. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026)

### Inferences
- The thumbnail still has to read clearly at phone size, where browsing and discovery often happen. It also has to read across a room on a TV. That favours 1–3 large words at most, high contrast, and one clear focal object or face.
- On-screen text such as dates, maps and labels should be large and stay up long enough to read from a sofa. Do not put key information in small lower-thirds.
- Viewers watching on TV play videos back to back and rely on autoplay. End screens and a consistent series format are what keep them in a session.
- Because CTV ad breaks are long and often unskippable, longer runtimes earn more per view on TV. This is a reason to aim for the upper end of 8–15 minutes once retention holds; mid-roll ads are allowed from 8 minutes.

### Gaps
- I found no device split specific to educational or history content. YouTube Studio shows this per channel.
- I found no hard Canadian device split.

## 3. When they watch: days, times, evenings, weekends, sleep and background viewing

### Takeaway
Upload-time studies point to weekday early afternoons, about 1–4 pm Eastern, so that a video is indexed before evening peak viewing, and to weekend mornings. These studies are weak and come from vendors. A large "history to fall asleep to" audience exists for ultra-long, low-stimulation history videos. It is a different use case from 8–15 minute myth-busting videos, and much of its supply is now AI-made.

### Cited Findings
- **Vendor/blog consensus (2025, low reliability):** Upload on weekdays at about 2–4 pm EST and on weekends at 9–11 am EST. Sprout Social points to about 1 pm on Mondays and Tuesdays. Buffer analysed 1 million videos for its timing guide. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/?p=199402); [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube?country=85)
- **Ofcom:** 84% of in-home video viewing in the UK was on the TV set in 2024. — [Ofcom Media Nations 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/media-nations-2025)
- **Sleep and bedtime history niche (third-party tool data, OutlierKit):** "Sleepless Historian" uploads almost only long-form videos averaging about 2 h 04 min. Titles that begin "Fall Asleep to…" or say "Documentary for Sleep" signal low-cognitive-load, overnight viewing. OutlierKit describes copycat competition as intense. — [OutlierKit: Sleepless Historian](https://outlierkit.com/channel/sleeplesshistorian); [OutlierKit: Bedtime History](https://outlierkit.com/channel/thebedtimehistory-icdi)
- **Press on AI sleep-history channels:** Reporting describes viewers falling asleep to AI-generated "boring history" videos. Some contain glitches and errors, and they are crowding out human historians. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/people-are-going-to-sleep-while-listening-to-ai-generated-trash-on-youtube/); [Der Standard (German), about Sep 2025](https://www.derstandard.at/story/3000000287806/kigeschichtskanaele-verdraengen-echte-historiker-auf-youtube); [The Note](https://thenote.app/post/en/ai-generated-boring-history-videos-are-flooding-youtube-drowning-out-real-7uwioqcezp)
- **Background and listen-style viewing (official):** YouTube says it is the most-used podcast service in the US, citing Edison Research, and reports more than 1 billion monthly podcast users. Many viewers "want to watch" podcasts rather than only listen. — [Tubefilter on Mohan letter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/11/neal-mohan-youtube-letter-2025-youtube-tv-watch-time-creator-studio-businesses/); [YouTube Culture & Trends, 20th anniversary report](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/trends-report-20th/)

### Inferences
- A practical test is to publish on Tuesdays–Thursdays around 12–3 pm ET, which is 5–8 pm in the UK and early morning the next day in eastern Australia. US evening viewing then lands in the first 24 hours. After about 20 uploads, switch to the "When your viewers are on YouTube" report in Studio.
- Weekend and evening viewing on TV suits the "Inside the Moment" first-person story format. The narration has to work as audio alone, because part of the audience half-watches. The script should never depend on reading the screen to follow the argument.
- "Sleep history" is a separate and saturated, largely AI-made market. Do not chase it with the 8–15 minute format. A later option is to stitch existing episodes into 1–3 hour compilations.

### Gaps
- I found no hard data, from Ofcom, Nielsen or YouTube, on viewing by hour for educational or history content.
- I found no figures on what share of history viewing happens at bedtime or in the background.
- Upload-time studies are vendor blogs with unclear methods and do not separate long-form education.

## 4. Preferred lengths; long-form (20–60+ min) vs 8–15 min; retention and AVD benchmarks

### Takeaway
Ultra-long videos of 2 hours or more boomed in 2024 but were hit hard by a January 2025 algorithm change. Little Dot reports ultra-long viewing fell more than 90% within three months. By 2025, views per video had evened out across lengths. Retention benchmarks for education run from about 30% of the video watched (vidIQ's conservative figure for 6–9 minute videos) to about 42% (Retention Rabbit's category average). These are vendor numbers and not independent.

### Cited Findings
- **Little Dot whitepaper (Feb 2026, 800+ channels, 11.2 billion monthly views):** In 2023–2024, videos over 120 minutes earned disproportionately more watch time, views and RPM. A 120-minute video averaged about 100 times the revenue of a 20-minute one. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026)
- **Same source:** After a significant algorithm update in January 2025, ultra-long viewing fell more than 90% from its 2024 average within three months. The average length of recommended videos fell from about 80 to about 40 minutes. The most popular videos on YouTube fell 21% in average length, from about 35 to about 28 minutes, between December 2024 and May 2025. In the 2025 steady state, views per video evened out across short, long and ultra-long formats. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026)
- **Long-form history channel benchmarks (OutlierKit estimates):** "The Entire History" videos average about 2 h 02 min, and one compilation reached 8.6 million views. Long-form makes up 83% of Epic History's views. — [OutlierKit: The Entire History](https://outlierkit.com/channel/theentirehistorydocumentaries); [OutlierKit: Epic History](https://outlierkit.com/channel/epichistorytv)
- **Retention, platform-wide (Retention Rabbit 2025, vendor, cited secondhand):** The average video keeps about 23.7% retention, and 55% of viewers leave within the first 60 seconds. The educational and how-to category averages 42.1%, the highest of any category. — [TubeLab](https://tubelab.net/blog/youtube-audience-retention)
- **Retention, conservative view (vidIQ):** vidIQ argues the common "50% or more is good" benchmark is inflated. It suggests about 30% of the video watched is typical for a 6–9 minute education video. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)
- **Other vendor ranges:** About 50–60% for 5–10 minute education videos (Prepublish.ai via Webtonic). FluxNote gives a 40–60% target. — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/education-coaching-video-marketing-statistics); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-average-view-duration-2026)
- **Method note:** Compare against your own videos of similar length, not a global benchmark. — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/elearning-video/articles-elearning/video-engagement-watch-time-drop-off)

### Inferences
- 8–15 minutes is a viable length after 2025. The algorithm's tilt toward multi-hour videos has eased, and mid-roll ads are allowed from 8 minutes. A realistic retention goal is about 35–45% of the video watched, which gives about 3.5–6 minutes of average view duration on a 10–13 minute video. More important is keeping the first-60-second drop below about 30%.
- Myth-busting fits retention well. Opening on the myth or the "what if" premise and holding back the answer creates an open loop.
- Long-form history (20–60 minutes or more) still performs for established channels such as Epic History and Kings and Generals, but the evidence is anecdotal and comes from tools. Later, two or three related episodes could be bundled into a 30–45 minute "season" compilation.

### Gaps
- I found no hard, YouTube-published benchmarks for retention or average view duration by category or length.
- I found no independent data on how 20–60 minute history documentaries perform against 8–15 minute videos specifically in 2025–2026. The Little Dot buckets are short, long and ultra-long (120+ minutes) and do not isolate 20–60 minutes.

## 5. Shorts vs long-form; does a Shorts audience convert?

### Takeaway
Shorts generate most of YouTube's views. Tubular reports more than 75% of views came from Shorts in 2025. They convert to subscribers and long-form viewers much less efficiently. One vidIQ data point needed about 505 Shorts views per new subscriber. Research on whether Shorts help or hurt long-form is correlational and mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **Tubular Labs (2025):** More than 75% of YouTube views came from Shorts in 2025. — [TVREV, Tube Trends](https://www.tvrev.com/news/tube-trends-over-75-of-youtube-views-came-from-shorts-in-2025-tubular)
- **AIR Media-Tech (18,000 channels, Mar 2025–Mar 2026, correlational):** Long-form leads subscriber growth in several niches. The authors warn that channels posting Shorts may simply be more active posters overall. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- **vidIQ's own channel (single creator, secondhand):** Shorts needed about 505 views per subscriber, compared with much better conversion from long-form. The secondhand phrasing of the long-form figure, "106 subscribers per view", is garbled and should not be quoted. — [vidIQ via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/the-fastest-way-to-1k-subs-in-2026)
- **Zebracat figure (via a blog, unverified):** 74% of Shorts views come from non-subscribers. — [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/convert-shorts-viewers-to-subscribers)
- **Industry study:** Views are rising while watch time and ad revenue fall, a pattern attributed to the Shorts mix. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-views-are-rising-but-watch-time-and-ad-revenue-are-falling-study-finds/)
- **Unverified claim to avoid quoting:** "Subscribers who arrive from Shorts have 4× higher long-form watch time." I found no primary source for it. — (flagged in search; no source)

### Inferences
- Use Shorts as trailers that point to a specific long video ("the myth in 45 seconds, the full answer in the video", using the related-video link). They should not be the growth engine for a long-form "what if" channel.
- Shorts subscribers tend to be weaker long-form viewers, and a large Shorts-acquired base can depress the click-through rate on long uploads. This is creator lore, not proven. Keep Shorts to about 2–3 per long video and keep them on-topic.

### Gaps
- YouTube has published no hard data on conversion from Shorts to long-form viewing.
- I found no history-specific Shorts data.

## 6. Attitudes: trust, sourcing, AI voices and visuals ("AI slop"), comment culture

### Takeaway
Distrust of AI content is growing. 58% of consumers say they would trust a brand less if it used AI voices, and 77% trust human voices most (Adobe). Press and historians have singled out AI history channels for errors. YouTube's July 2025 policy clarification targets "mass-produced, repetitive" content, not AI use itself. A faceless channel's best protection is visible sourcing, human editorial judgement and an original script.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube Partner Program update, effective July 15, 2025:** YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" to better catch mass-produced, repetitive videos. Rene Ritchie described it as a "minor update", saying such content was already ineligible to earn money. AI-assisted content can still be monetized if it meets the other policies. — [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/07/10/youtube-is-fighting-ai-slop-with-new-monetization-guidelines/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-partner-monetization-policy-changes-3575999); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)
- **Adobe Express survey (AI voice):** 88% of consumers have heard a voice and wondered whether it was AI. 77% trust human voices most. 58% would trust a brand less for using AI voices in ads. Nearly three in four want AI voice and music disclosed. These figures are about brands and advertising, not YouTube viewers specifically. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/ai-audio-consumer-trust)
- **Animoto (Sep 2025, n=460, US):** Respondents most often spot AI through "unnatural voice patterns", robotic gestures and a lack of emotional tone. 36% said AI-generated videos reduced their trust in a brand. About 83% said they had watched a video they suspected was AI (State of Video 2026). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/consumers-detect-ai-generated-video-content-while-marketers-embrace-tools-study-finds/)
- **Story Radius (Nov–Dec 2025, n=512 US adults):** Most Americans are not excited about the rise of AI video on social media, and many say it weakens trust and emotional engagement. — [national survey release](https://lifestyle.rewind1019.com/story/53320743/new-national-survey-finds-americans-are-turning-against-ai-generated-video-on-social-media)
- **Pew (Sep 2025):** Most Americans say it is important to be able to tell AI-made content from human-made content, but many doubt they can do it themselves. — (summarised in search results; Pew AI report, not fetched)
- **History-specific backlash:** AI-narrated history clips are drawing views and are "full of false information", while channels by real historians, such as The Great War / Real Time History and History Time, are being crowded out. — [Der Standard](https://www.derstandard.at/story/3000000287806/kigeschichtskanaele-verdraengen-echte-historiker-auf-youtube); [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/people-are-going-to-sleep-while-listening-to-ai-generated-trash-on-youtube/)

### Inferences
- Faceless is not the problem; looking mass-produced is. Distinguishing signals include a consistent human-sounding narrator (a real voice actor or a carefully directed, licensed synthetic voice, labelled where required) and original scripts with a clear argument. Others are real archival images and maps with credits, a sources list in the description, and on-screen citations at contested claims.
- Myth-busting audiences reward rigour and punish errors. Expect "well, actually" corrections in the comments. Pinned correction comments and "Sources and corrections" sections build trust and engagement. Comment debate also lifts engagement signals.
- For "Inside the Moment" first-person storytelling, label dramatised parts clearly, for example "dramatised reconstruction based on [source]", so the channel is not accused of inventing history.
- Avoid AI imagery that is obviously anachronistic, such as wrong uniforms or garbled text. History audiences notice these details and call them out.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of YouTube history or education viewers specifically on AI narration. All AI-attitude data is general-public or brand-focused.
- I found no quantitative study of comment culture (correction or debate rates) on history channels. Evidence is anecdotal.
- I did not find an original Pew survey figure on trust in YouTube educational content.

## 7. Platform reports overview (YouTube Culture & Trends, Think with Google, Pew, Ofcom, Nielsen)

### Takeaway
Official sources confirm the big structural facts: YouTube leads TV viewing in the US, ranks #2 in the UK, has growing TV share in Australia, and is #1 among streamers in Canada. They also confirm podcast and long-listening behaviour and a learning motive. None publishes history-genre audience data.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube Culture & Trends 2025 (20th anniversary report):** YouTube reports more than 1 billion monthly podcast users and more than 100 million Premium subscribers. The "Creative Maximalism" Gen Z survey sampled 500 people aged 14–24 and 500 aged 25–49 in the US, in April 2025. — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/trends-report-20th/); [Tubefilter 2025 Culture & Trends](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/15/youtube-2025-culture-and-trends/amp/)
- **Think with Google (older):** 500 million views of learning-related content every day, and 1 million learning videos shared daily (2016–2018 vintage). — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/data/learning-related-youtube-content-statistics)
- **Nielsen, Ofcom, OzTAM and Numeris:** see sections 2 and 1.

### Inferences
- For a pitch or strategy document, the most defensible headline figures are YouTube's 12.4% peak share of US TV time (Nielsen, April 2025) and TV as the #1 US screen (YouTube). Add Ofcom's 41% of UK in-home YouTube viewing on TV (2024) and YouTube's 11.3% of Australian TV-set video viewing (OzTAM, Q2 2026).

### Gaps
- Think with Google learning statistics are 2016–2018 data. I found no updated 2024–2026 official learning statistics.
- I did not fetch Nielsen's 2025–2026 Gauge reports for later months; the 2026 monthly share is not captured here.
- No public analysis by Paddy Galloway focuses on history or documentary niches. His published method is outlier analysis: study the videos that performed 5–15 times above a channel's baseline. — [OutlierKit on Galloway](https://outlierkit.com/resources/paddy-galloway-growth-strategy/)
